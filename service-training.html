AlphaDog Assist

Service dogs, trained for real life

Our Assist program was founded and is led by Sierra Berrick, our Service Dog Training Director and a mental health specialist, along with Mike Wombacher, expert dog trainer. The program prepares dogs for real life and teaches them to provide life-changing support for their handlers.

What we train for

Purpose-built partnerships

AlphaDog Assist trains dogs to help with a range of real-world needs. We train the following types of service dogs:

  • Psychiatric service: deep pressure therapy, stress signal recognition, panic attack mitigation, interruption
  • Medical alert: medication reminder, medication retrieval, nightmare interruption, hearing alert
  • Migraine alert
  • Allergen alert
  • Mobility assistance: bracing, fall recovery and prevention, pulling up hills, retrieval, tug, counterbalance

How it works

From evaluation to working partner

1

Consultation

We learn about your needs, your situation, and, if you already have a dog, whether they're a good candidate for service dog work.

2

Task training

Your dog trains with our team on the specific tasks you depend on, built on a rock-solid obedience foundation.

3

Handler transfer

We train you and your dog together, so the partnership works in the real world, not just at our facility.

4

Graduation

The handler and dog complete a real-world outing that includes indoor dining, public transportation, and task work in challenging environments. After completion, the team receives a letter of attestation for training (this is separate from the letter teams will need to acquire from their doctor or therapist) and is considered fully trained.

Service dog FAQ

Common questions about AlphaDog Assist

What kinds of service dogs do you train?
We train psychiatric service dogs (deep pressure therapy, stress signal recognition, panic attack mitigation, and interruption), medical alert dogs (medication reminders and retrieval, nightmare interruption, and hearing alert), migraine alert, allergen alert, and mobility assistance dogs (bracing, fall recovery and prevention, pulling up hills, retrieval, tug, and counterbalance).
Does a vest make a dog a service dog?
No. A vest is just gear. It can signal that a dog is working and shouldn't be distracted, but it doesn't give a dog any abilities on its own. What makes a service dog is the trained task work: the specific, reliable tasks the dog performs to support its handler. That task training, built on a solid obedience foundation, is what our program focuses on.
Can you train a dog I already have?
Possibly. Every partnership starts with a consultation, where we learn about your needs and, if you already have a dog, assess whether they're a good candidate for service dog work.
What if a service dog can't help with my condition?
That's part of what the consultation is for. Not every need is a fit for service dog work, and we'll be honest with you about whether a trained dog is the right solution for your situation. If it isn't, we'd rather tell you upfront than start a program that won't serve you.
Who leads the program?
AlphaDog Assist was founded and is led by Sierra Berrick, our service dog training director and mental health specialist, alongside Mike Wombacher, our expert dog trainer.
Do you train the handler too, or just the dog?
Both. During the handler transfer stage we train you and your dog together, so the partnership works out in the real world, not just at our facility.
How long does the training take?
It depends. Every dog and every set of tasks is different, so timelines vary with your dog's starting point, the tasks involved, and how training progresses. We'll give you a realistic sense of the timeline during your consultation.
What happens at graduation, and is it a certification?
To graduate, the handler and dog complete a real-world outing that includes indoor dining, public transportation, and task work in challenging environments. The team then receives a letter of attestation for training. That's separate from the letter you'll need to obtain from your own doctor or therapist.
How do I book a consultation?
Every service dog partnership starts with a conversation. Reach out at (415) 383-2111 or dogs@alphadoglodging.com and we'll schedule a consultation. You can also download the AlphaDog Assist brochure to learn more first.
